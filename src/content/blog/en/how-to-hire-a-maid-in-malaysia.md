Hiring a domestic helper is one of the most personal decisions a family makes. She will live in your home, care for the people you love and become part of your daily routine. Planning carefully before you start makes the whole process smoother, for your family and for her.

This guide walks through the decisions you will need to make, in the order you will usually make them.

## 1. Decide what your household really needs

Start with the work, not the nationality. Write down:

- **Who needs care.** A newborn, young children, an elderly parent, or nobody in particular?
- **The main duties.** Childcare, elderly care, cooking, cleaning, laundry, or a mix.
- **Your home.** The size of the home, number of people living there, and whether you have pets.
- **Your routine.** Who is home during the day, and when would you most need help?
- **Household considerations.** Religion, dietary practices and the languages spoken at home.

Be realistic about combining roles. A helper caring full-time for a bed-bound parent cannot also keep a large house spotless. Clear priorities help you choose the right person and avoid disappointment later.

## 2. Choose between an Indonesian and a Filipino helper

Malaysian families commonly hire helpers from Indonesia and the Philippines. Each person is an individual, so experience and attitude matter more than nationality. Still, there are practical differences:

- **Language.** Many Indonesian helpers speak Bahasa Indonesia, which is close to Bahasa Malaysia. Many Filipino helpers communicate in English.
- **Procedures.** Each country has its own recruitment rules, documentation and contract requirements.
- **Package terms.** Prices, inclusions and terms may differ between nationalities.

Our article on [Indonesian vs Filipino maids](../indonesian-maid-vs-filipino-maid/) looks at this in more detail.

## 3. Work with a licensed agency

A licensed private employment agency helps you find candidates, explains current requirements and handles much of the paperwork. When comparing agencies, ask:

- Is the agency licensed, and what is its licence number?
- Can you see biodata and interview candidates before deciding?
- What exactly is included in the package price, and what is not?
- What are the warranty and replacement terms, in writing?
- Who will be your contact person after the helper arrives?

Avoid anyone who promises guaranteed approvals or fixed arrival dates. Approvals are decided by the authorities, and no agency can guarantee them.

## 4. Review biodata and interview candidates

A biodata summarises a candidate's experience, skills and languages. Use it to create a shortlist, then interview your shortlisted candidates — usually by video call. Our guide on [how to interview a domestic helper](../how-to-interview-a-domestic-helper/) includes questions that help you understand real experience.

## 5. Prepare your documents

Once you choose a candidate, you will need to provide documents for the application. These usually include your identification, proof of income and proof of address, and may include other documents depending on your household and the helper's nationality. See [documents needed to hire a domestic helper](../documents-needed-to-hire-a-domestic-helper/).

## 6. Applications, approvals and arrival

Your application is submitted to the relevant authorities. After approval, the helper's travel is arranged. When she arrives in Malaysia, she must complete a medical examination at a FOMEMA-registered clinic, and the remaining work-pass formalities are completed.

How long this takes depends on documents, approvals and travel. Ask your agency for an estimate, and treat it as an estimate.

## 7. Handover and settling in

Before handover, a good agency provides an orientation for the helper. The first weeks in your home are just as important: explain routines patiently and agree on expectations early. Read [what happens after your maid arrives](../what-happens-after-your-maid-arrives-in-malaysia/) for a practical first-month plan.

## Quick checklist

- List your household's needs and priorities
- Choose a preferred nationality, or ask for candidates from both
- Confirm the agency's licence and package terms in writing
- Interview your shortlisted candidates
- Prepare your documents early
- Plan the first weeks after arrival
