Most difficulties between families and helpers can be traced back to a few avoidable decisions made at the start. Here are the most common mistakes we see, and how to avoid them.

## 1. Not being clear about the job

"General housework" means different things to different families. If you need help mainly with a newborn, or an elderly parent, say so clearly and choose a candidate with that experience. Write down the main duties before you start looking.

## 2. Expecting one person to do everything

A single helper cannot provide full-time care for a bed-bound parent, look after two toddlers and keep a large house spotless at the same time. Prioritise. If your needs are very heavy, discuss them honestly with your agency.

## 3. Skipping or rushing the interview

The interview is your best chance to understand a candidate. Prepare open questions, describe your home honestly and give her time to ask questions. See [how to interview a domestic helper](../how-to-interview-a-domestic-helper/).

## 4. Choosing an agency on price alone

A low headline price can leave out important items, or come with strict replacement terms. Before you commit, ask for the **full inclusions, exclusions, warranty and replacement terms in writing**, and check that the agency is licensed. Compare [our packages](../../maid-packages/) on what is actually included.

## 5. Believing guaranteed timelines

Approvals are decided by government authorities, and travel depends on the helper's own documents. Be cautious of anyone who guarantees an approval or a fixed arrival date. Plan with a buffer, especially if you need help by a certain date, such as the birth of a baby.

## 6. Starting the paperwork late

Missing or unclear documents cause delays. Prepare your documents as soon as you decide to hire — see [documents needed to hire a domestic helper](../documents-needed-to-hire-a-domestic-helper/). The same applies to renewals: start well before the permit expires.

## 7. Neglecting the first few weeks

A new helper is adjusting to a new country, a new language and a new family. Clear instructions, patience, proper rest and respectful communication in the first weeks make a big difference to how well she settles. Read [what happens after your maid arrives](../what-happens-after-your-maid-arrives-in-malaysia/).

## The bottom line

Be clear, be realistic, ask for terms in writing, and invest time at the start. Families who do this usually have far smoother placements.
